\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon vision--language--action policies must decide both which subgoal to execute and how long to continue each predicted motion. Whole-task captions and fixed action chunks provide neither decision explicitly. We introduce event-aligned spline action programs, an interface in which ordered language clauses select the current subgoal and each policy query predicts a short command path separately from its duration. In a controlled study crossing two action heads and two label types on LIBERO-Long, training with clauses improves success under an identical clause schedule by 34.3 percentage points for a waypoint head and 43.7 points for the spline head. The resulting 9.3-point interaction is positive for all three training seeds. Reversing the clause order changes the first completed subgoal, while semantic phase labels improve aggregate success on two longer RoboCasa365 tasks in two independent training runs. The same shape--duration representation also exposes a physical clock. Retiming a frozen spline policy raises mean CALVIN chain length from 1.31 to 1.69 across three seeds at 1.42$\times$ realized pace, and replanning from predicted duration preserves success on two LIBERO suites while reducing policy queries by 2.2--2.7$\times$. The results show how one compact action interface can support explicit semantic and temporal control without modifying the vision--language backbone.
\end{abstract}
