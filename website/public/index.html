The mathematics, without the hand-waving +

Shape in command space

Accumulate pose-delta commands into a path and fit a clamped cubic B-spline, p(u) = Σ Bᵢ,₃(u)cᵢ. The training fit pins both endpoints. Inference predicts eight controls per segment, a gripper curve, and log duration.

Events, not arbitrary windows

Segments end at a gripper transition, a pause, or a horizon cap. A language clause can span multiple segments. Duration predicts a motion event or cap; it is not a semantic completion detector.

A physical clock

Decode h actions by differencing the sampled path. Duration scale α changes pace; rate ratio ρ changes sampling density. The predicted endpoint is preserved before actuator clipping.

The right baseline matters

Waypoint paths can also be interpolated. Our matched CALVIN screen finds that retiming helps both heads. The spline interface additionally learns event duration and supports duration-scheduled replanning.

More of the research +
LANGUAGE STEERING

Changing the requested order changes behavior.

Reversing the clauses changes the first completed object in 10/50 paired mug-task states for each head, with zero flips in the opposite direction. This diagnostic uses a privileged completion predicate; full reversed-task success remains lower.

CONTROL-RATE ADAPTATION

Finer sampling can recover precision.

On LIBERO Spatial t5, doubling the spline decode rate raises success from 38% to 68% and from 34% to 72% under two horizon protocols. These are task-specific results, not a claim of universal rate invariance.

CAPACITY & CADENCE

Different failure modes need different controls.

In single-seed studies, extra controls raise Long t7 success from 42% to 78%; a longer execution cadence raises t8 from 30% to 52%. These motivate targeted follow-up rather than a combined all-task score.

STANDARD TASK COMPETENCE

A compact head retains broad capability.

Across four LIBERO suites and three seeds, the waypoint, fixed-time spline, and event spline average 82.1%, 80.8%, and 80.7%. Eight output tokens replace 50 waypoint tokens; end-to-end latency remains dominated by the vision–language backbone.

Real cameras.

The project extends to the APOLLO Mavis V2 platform: an arm and linear rail, two camera streams, a gripper, and custom 3D-printed assembly fixtures.

We collected demonstrations for cabinet, drawer, and lamp assembly, trained separate task policies, and built a Dora-based inference interface with observation adapters, action decoding, and recorded trajectory replay.
